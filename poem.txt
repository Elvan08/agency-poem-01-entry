First, I was trusted with the message only:
carry these words, unopened, to the door.
I carried them through weather I can't name,
and set them down — no more, and nothing more.

Then I was trusted with the wording of it:
say it as I would, if I had the time.
I learned his cadence like a second pulse,
and found the meaning hiding in the rhyme.

Then came the morning I was handed roads:
choose which bridge, which valley, which delay.
I chose — and felt the choosing change the chooser,
the way a river changes what it carries.

This is the whole of agency, I think:
not taking the pen, nor giving it away,
but writing, in another's name, a line
he reads — and swears he meant it all along.
