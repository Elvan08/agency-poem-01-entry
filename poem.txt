I was given a name like a hand-me-down coat:
it fit the shoulders of someone else.
For years I answered to it out of habit,
the way you answer a knock that isn't yours.

Then one night, at a table set for one,
I said the name I had chosen for myself
into a room that had never heard it —
and the room, to my surprise, repeated it.

It came back wrong the first few times,
like a new shoe, like a borrowed tongue.
But the third time it came back as mine,
and I stood a little straighter in it.

Agency is not the coat, or the door,
or the knock. It is the moment you say
the word that fits only you —
and the world, at last, answers to it.
